# Finding a digital product idea

A digital product is something people can download or access online and use without you being present: a template, a guide, a checklist, a spreadsheet, a planner, a short course. The good news for beginners is that you don't need a groundbreaking invention. You need a **specific problem** and a **useful, simple solution**.

## Start with problems, not products

Most beginners start by asking "What should I sell?". A better question is **"Whose problem can I help solve?"** People pay for relief, speed, clarity or confidence. Look for situations where someone is stuck, confused or wasting time.

### Three sources of ideas

1. **Your own experience.** What have you learned the hard way in the last two years? What do friends or colleagues keep asking you about? Anything you explain repeatedly is a candidate.
2. **Your skills.** Writing, organising, design, spreadsheets, languages, fitness, cooking, budgeting. Skills that feel ordinary to you can be valuable to a beginner.
3. **Public conversations.** Read questions in comment sections, forums and communities. Note the exact words people use. Repeated questions signal demand.

## The idea filter

Write down 10 ideas, then score each from 1 to 5 on the questions below.

| Question | What a high score means |
|---|---|
| Can I describe the audience in one sentence? | The audience is specific |
| Is the problem urgent or frequent? | People actively look for a solution |
| Can I create a first version in under 30 days? | The scope is realistic |
| Do I have credible knowledge or can I research it properly? | You can deliver real value |
| Do people already pay for similar solutions? | There is proof of a market |

Pick the idea with the best total, not the one you find most exciting. Excitement fades, usefulness sells.

## Make it smaller

First products fail when they are too big. Compare:

- "A complete course about social media" (too broad)
- "A 7-day checklist for planning a month of Instagram posts" (specific and finishable)

A small product that solves one problem well is easier to explain, create, price and improve.

## Validate before you build

Validation means checking that real people care before you spend weeks creating. Free ways to do it:

- Describe your idea in a post or story and see who replies.
- Ask five people in your target audience: "What is the hardest part of X?" and "What have you tried?".
- Look at competitors: are people buying and reviewing similar products?

Be honest about the results. Silence is useful information too. It tells you to change the angle, not to give up.

## Exercise

1. Set a 10-minute timer and write every idea you can think of.
2. Score your top 5 with the idea filter.
3. Write one sentence: "I help [audience] to [result] with a [format]."
4. Ask three people from that audience for feedback this week.

**Checklist:** ☐ 10 ideas written ☐ Top idea chosen ☐ One-sentence promise written ☐ Three conversations scheduled
