# Understanding basic business finances

This chapter is general educational information, not legal, tax or accounting advice. Rules differ by country and change over time. Speak with a qualified professional where you live before making financial decisions.

## Key terms

- **Revenue:** the total money customers pay you.
- **Costs:** what you spend to run the business (tools, fees, advertising, software, domain).
- **Profit:** revenue minus costs.
- **Cash flow:** money coming in and out over time.
- **Payment fees:** payment providers charge a fee per transaction.

### A simple example

A customer pays €19.99. After payment fees, tools and a share of your monthly costs, you may keep much less than the price. That's why tracking matters.

## Set up basic tracking

Create a spreadsheet or Notion database with columns: date, description, category, amount, type (income or expense). Update it weekly. Keep invoices and receipts according to local rules.

## Pricing with costs in mind

List your monthly costs and estimate how many sales you'd need to cover them. Don't forget your time. Even if you can't pay yourself yet, knowing the number helps you decide when to invest more.

## Taxes and registration

Selling online is often a taxable activity. Depending on the country you may need to register as a business, charge VAT or sales tax (especially on digital goods sold to consumers in other countries), keep records and file returns. Check official government websites or consult an accountant before you start selling.

## Separate your money

Where possible, use a separate account for business income and expenses. It simplifies bookkeeping.

## Set aside money for taxes

Move a portion of each sale into a savings account so you aren't surprised later. Ask a professional what percentage is appropriate for your situation.

## Be realistic

Many beginners earn little at first. Treat the first months as a time to learn, test and improve. Avoid taking on debt for tools or advertising before you have evidence that customers want your product.

## Monthly money routine (20 minutes)

1. Record all income and expenses.
2. Compare to last month.
3. Cancel tools you don't use.
4. Note one lesson and one action.

**Checklist:** ☐ Tracker created ☐ Costs listed ☐ Registration requirements checked ☐ Tax advice sought ☐ Monthly routine scheduled
