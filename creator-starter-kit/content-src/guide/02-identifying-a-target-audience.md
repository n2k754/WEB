# Identifying a target audience

"Everyone" is not an audience. When you try to speak to everyone, your message becomes vague and nobody feels it was written for them. A clear target audience makes every decision easier: what to create, how to price it, where to post and which words to use.

## Describe a person, not a demographic

Age and location are rarely enough. What matters is the **situation** and the **struggle**. Use this structure:

> My audience is **[who]** who want to **[goal]** but are struggling with **[obstacle]**.

Example: "Part-time creators who want to post consistently but are overwhelmed by planning."

## The four layers of understanding

1. **Situation:** Who are they? What stage are they at (complete beginner, intermediate)? How much time and money do they have?
2. **Goals:** What are they trying to achieve in the next 3 to 6 months?
3. **Obstacles:** What stops them? Time, confidence, knowledge, tools, money?
4. **Language:** Which exact words do they use to describe the problem? Use those words in your marketing.

## How to research without a big audience

- **Read comments** under popular videos and posts in your niche. Copy the recurring questions into a document.
- **Join communities** where your audience asks for help and observe before posting. Respect each community's rules and don't spam.
- **Talk to people.** Five short conversations beat fifty assumptions. Ask open questions: "What was the hardest part last time you tried this?"
- **Use search suggestions.** Type a topic into a search bar and note the suggested completions. They show what people actually ask.

## Avoid common mistakes

- **Choosing an audience you don't understand** because it seems profitable.
- **Assuming you are your audience.** You may share some traits, but check.
- **Making the audience too narrow too early.** Specific is good; so narrow that nobody exists is not.
- **Never updating the profile.** Revisit it every few weeks as you learn.
